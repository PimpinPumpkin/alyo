Alyo is speech for your phone without Google: voice typing in any app, and a recorder that writes down what is said while you are still talking. Everything happens on the phone: no account, no cloud, no Google services.

Turn on either part or both. They share the same speech models, so those are downloaded once.

<b>Voice typing</b>

• The microphone key on your keyboard opens it, in any app.
• Words appear as you speak, with punctuation and capitals added for you.
• Say "comma", "period" or "new line" if you prefer.
• Undo the last sentence or backspace a word without leaving the panel.
• Also answers apps that ask Android for speech.

<b>Recorder</b>

• Live transcript: words appear as you speak, then each sentence is rewritten with punctuation once you pause.
• Linked audio and text: the word being played is highlighted, and tapping a word jumps to it.
• Speaker labels you can rename, merge and correct.
• Fix mistakes: tap a sentence to correct it.
• Crop and remove: select by dragging across the words, the waveform or the seek bar.
• Summaries: a small language model on the phone can write a title and the key points. Optional, and a separate download.
• Music, applause and laughter are marked in the waveform and the transcript.
• Import audio files and transcribe them.
• Back up and restore the whole library.
• Widget and quick settings tile to start recording in one tap.
• Search across every transcript, and within one.
• Export audio (M4A), transcript (TXT), subtitles (SRT) or per-word timings (JSON).

<b>Offline</b>

The app goes online for one thing only: downloading the speech model the first time, and the summary model if you want one. You can import those files yourself instead, and then it never needs the network at all.

<b>Models</b>

Speech recognition uses NVIDIA's FastConformer and Parakeet models and Banafo's Kroko models, through sherpa-onnx. Summaries use Qwen2.5 through llama.cpp. The recorder transcribes English, German, French and Spanish; voice typing is English for now.
